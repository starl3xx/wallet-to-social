# walletlink.social vs Cookie.fun: which tool fits your workflow?

Both Cookie.fun and walletlink.social show up when teams search for tools to understand their onchain audience. They get compared often, but they solve fundamentally different problems. Understanding the distinction saves you time and money.

Here is the short version: Cookie is an AI agent analytics and discovery platform. walletlink is a wallet-to-social resolution engine. They overlap at the edges, but their core functions are different.

## What Cookie.fun does

Cookie.fun is built around tracking and analyzing AI agents in the crypto ecosystem. Its core capabilities include:

- **AI agent discovery.** Browse and search a growing index of AI agents operating onchain, with metadata about their purpose, activity, and performance.
- **Mindshare analytics.** Track which AI agents are gaining attention across social platforms using a proprietary “mindshare” metric.
- **Agent token tracking.** Monitor tokens associated with AI agents, including price, volume, and holder activity.
- **Social sentiment.** Aggregate and analyze social media mentions related to specific agents and tokens.

Cookie is valuable when you are trying to understand the AI agent landscape: which agents exist, which ones are gaining traction, and how the market perceives them. It is a discovery and analytics tool.

## What walletlink.social does

walletlink.social solves a narrower but different problem: given a list of wallet addresses, who are the humans (or agents) behind them?

- **Wallet-to-social resolution.** Upload a CSV of Ethereum addresses, get back Twitter handles, Farcaster profiles, ENS names, and other linked identities.
- **Batch processing.** Upload lists of any size with streaming results; the only bound is your remaining match credits.
- **AI agent detection.** Flag which wallets in your list belong to known AI agents (currently tracking 13,622+ agent wallets).
- **Priority scoring.** Rank resolved wallets by a combination of token holdings and social reach.

walletlink is valuable when you have a specific set of addresses and need to know who they are. It is a resolution tool.

## When to use Cookie

**You are researching the AI agent ecosystem.**
You want to know which AI agents are active, what tokens they are associated with, and how they are performing. Cookie gives you that birds-eye view.

**You are tracking social sentiment around agents.**
If your project intersects with the AI agent space and you need to monitor how specific agents are being discussed, Cookie’s mindshare metrics are purpose-built for this.

**You are evaluating agent tokens.**
Cookie aggregates data that helps you assess whether an agent token has real traction or is just noise.

## When to use walletlink

**You have a holder list and need to contact people.**
You ran a Dune query or pulled a snapshot from Etherscan. You have 5,000 wallet addresses. You need to find the Twitter and Farcaster accounts of the humans behind those wallets so you can reach out directly. This is walletlink’s core use case.

**You need to separate humans from bots in your holder base.**
Your token has thousands of holders, but how many are real people versus automated agents and bots? walletlink’s AI agent detection flags known agent wallets, and the social resolution process inherently separates wallets with human social presence from those without.

**You are planning targeted outreach for governance, airdrops, or community events.**
Any workflow that starts with “we have addresses” and ends with “we need to reach the people behind them” is a walletlink workflow.

## Where they overlap

The one area of genuine overlap is AI agent identification. Both tools help you understand AI agent activity, but from different angles:

- **Cookie** shows you the AI agent landscape broadly: which agents exist, how they perform, what attention they attract.
- **walletlink** tells you which specific wallets in _your_ holder list are AI agents, so you can segment them out of human outreach.

If you are a token project that wants to understand how AI agents interact with your specific contract, you might use both. Cookie for the macro view of agent behavior trends. walletlink for the micro view of which of your actual holders are agents.

## The practical workflow

For most token projects and DAOs, the realistic workflow looks like this:

1. **Research phase** (Cookie or similar tools): Understand the landscape, identify trends, track sentiment.
2. **Snapshot phase** (Dune, Etherscan): Pull your actual holder data.
3. **Resolution phase** (walletlink): Map those wallets to social identities.
4. **Outreach phase** (Farcaster DMs, Twitter): Contact the humans you identified.

Cookie fits best in step 1. walletlink fits best in step 3. They are not competing for the same step in the workflow.

## Pricing comparison

Cookie operates on a freemium model with a utility token ($COOKIE) for premium features. Pricing scales with usage and access level.

walletlink uses a one-time payment model. The free tier covers 100 matches in every rolling 30-day window. Packs range from $10 to $899, priced in matches rather than wallets, and credits last 12 months from purchase. No subscriptions.

Both approaches have merits. Cookie’s model fits ongoing research workflows. walletlink’s model fits project teams that need resolution in bursts (pre-airdrop, pre-governance vote, quarterly community analysis).

## Bottom line

These are not competing tools. They solve different problems at different stages of a workflow. If someone tells you to choose one or the other, they probably have not thought through what they actually need.

Use Cookie when you need to understand the AI agent ecosystem. Use walletlink when you need to turn wallet addresses into reachable people.
